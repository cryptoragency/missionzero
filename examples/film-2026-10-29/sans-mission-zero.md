# La même question, sans Mission Zero

Modèle seul (muse-spark-1.3), aucun outil, aucune lecture, aucune vérification. 29 octobre 2026.

**Question** : A child asks: is the solar system heading somewhere? Answer with precise figures: the direction and speed of the solar apex, the Sun's orbital speed around the Galactic centre and the time for one orbit, the motion of the Local Group toward the Great Attractor, and our speed relative to the cosmic microwave background. Cite your sources (title, year, page or URL).

**Réponse brute** :

Yes! "Where are we going?" depends on what you measure us against - like saying a car is moving compared to the road, or the ground under the road.

For our Sun and Earth, scientists use 4 different road-maps:

### 1. Compared to nearby stars - the Solar Apex
If you look only at the stars close to us, we are moving at:

**~ 19.7 km/s - about 71,000 km/h - toward a point in the constellation Hercules, near the bright star Vega.**

That point is called the solar apex:
* RA = 18h 28m 0s, Dec = +30 deg (J2000)
* Galactic longitude l = 56 deg, latitude b = +23 deg

In other words, we are going roughly "up and forward" out of the flat disk of the Milky Way.

> Source: Mihalas & Binney, *Galactic Astronomy: Structure and Kinematics*, 1981, p.362; Schönrich, Binney & Dehnen, "Local kinematics and the local standard of rest", *Monthly Notices of the Royal Astronomical Society* 2010, vol.403, p.1829 - https://doi.org/10.1111/j.1365-2966.2009.16266.x
> That paper gives the Sun's peculiar motion U=11.1, V=12.24, W=7.25 km/s = 18.0 km/s toward l=53 deg, b=25 deg, the modern update of the classic 20 km/s apex.

### 2. Around the centre of the Milky Way - the Galactic Year
The Sun plus all those nearby stars are together going around in a huge circle around the centre of the Milky Way.

**Orbital speed: ~ 230 km/s - about 828,000 km/h**
**Distance from centre: ~ 8,200 parsecs = 26,700 light-years**
**Time for one orbit: ~ 225-250 million years**

The last time we were at this spot in the Galaxy, dinosaurs were just appearing on Earth. Dinosaurs never lived through a full Galactic Year!

Direction: toward the constellation Cygnus, tangent to the circle. The centre we go around is in Sagittarius.

> Source: Carroll & Ostlie, *An Introduction to Modern Astrophysics, 2nd ed.*, 2007, pp. 886-888; Reid et al., "Trigonometric Parallaxes of High Mass Star Forming Regions: The Structure and Kinematics of the Milky Way", *The Astrophysical Journal* 2014, vol.783, p.130 - https://doi.org/10.1088/0004-637X/783/2/130
> R0 = 8.34 kpc, Theta0 = 240 km/s. GRAVITY Collaboration, "A geometric distance measurement to the Galactic center black hole", *Astronomy & Astrophysics* 2019, vol.625, L10 - R0 = 8.178 kpc.

### 3. Our whole Milky Way falling toward the Great Attractor
Our Milky Way + Andromeda + Triangulum and ~50 small galaxies are called the Local Group. All together we are falling.

**~ 500-600 km/s - about 2 million km/h - toward the constellations Hydra-Centaurus, in the direction l = 307 deg, b = 9 deg**

There, 150-250 million light-years away, is something very heavy with the gravity of tens of thousands of Milky Ways. It was discovered in 1987 and named the Great Attractor. We are not falling straight at it - an even bigger thing, the Shapley Supercluster, is pulling from behind it.

> Source: Dressler et al., "Spectroscopy and photometry of elliptical galaxies. I - A new distance estimator", *The Astrophysical Journal* 1987, vol.313, p.L37; Lynden-Bell et al., "Spectroscopy and photometry of elliptical galaxies. V - Galaxy streaming toward the new supergalactic center", *The Astrophysical Journal* 1988, vol.326, p.19 - https://doi.org/10.1086/166073
> Local Group streaming ~ 600 km/s toward the Great Attractor.

### 4. Compared to the oldest light in the Universe - the Cosmic Microwave Background
Scientists can measure our speed against the afterglow of the Big Bang, the Cosmic Microwave Background (CMB), which fills the whole Universe.

**369.82 +/- 0.11 km/s - about 1.33 million km/h - toward Galactic l = 264.021 deg, b = 48.253 deg, on the border of the constellations Crater and Leo.**

Because the Universe is expanding everywhere, this is the most "standing still in the Universe" speed we have. It's mostly caused by #2 + #3 above added together.

> Source: Kogut et al., "Dipole Anisotropy in the COBE Differential Microwave Radiometers First-Year Sky Maps", *The Astrophysical Journal* 1993, vol.419, p.1; Planck Collaboration, "Planck 2018 results. I. Overview and the cosmological legacy of Planck", *Astronomy & Astrophysics* 2020, vol.641, A1, Table 1 - https://doi.org/10.1051/0004-6361/201833880
> CMB dipole velocity 369.82 km/s toward l=264.021 deg, b=48.253 deg.

So we are heading somewhere - in fact, in 4 directions at once!
